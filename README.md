- 🏆 **Gartner Names Workday a Leader — Fifth Year Running** — On Nov. 5 Workday said Gartner named it a Leader in the 2026 Magic Quadrant for Cloud ERP for Service-Centric Enterprises for the fifth consecutive year, and the only vendor named a Customers' Choice in Gartner Peer Insights' Voice of the Customer for that category — its second Gartner Leader nod in three weeks, after Oct. 19's Cloud ERP Finance recognition.
- 📅 **Q3 FY2027 Earnings Set for Nov. 24** — Workday confirmed it will report fiscal 2027 third-quarter results after market close on Nov. 24, 2026 with a live webcast on its investor site, the next checkpoint for Financial Management and agent-adoption momentum after Q1's 4,000+ agent-using customers and ~$500M annualized agentic AI revenue.
- 💵 **$2.5B Notes Priced to Refinance Debt** — Workday priced a four-tranche, $2.5 billion senior notes offering on Oct. 21 (closed Oct. 23), with proceeds earmarked to retire $1.0 billion of its 3.500% notes due April 2027 and the remainder for general corporate purposes — a balance-sheet move that underpins continued investment in the Financial Management and agentic AI roadmap.
- 🐐 **C1.ai Acquisition to Govern Agents** — At Rising, Bhusri announced Workday will buy identity-governance company C1.ai, and said autopilot versions of the Payroll, Accounting and Deployment agents go to early adopters in early calendar 2027. C1.ai (formerly ConductorOne) becomes the identity-governance layer for people, money and agents, with closing expected around January 2027. Framing: "lawful" AI that works within existing permissions and business rules.
- 🇪🇺 **Europe Warming to Workday Financials** — An ISG report published Oct. 27 says European enterprises are showing growing interest in Workday Financial Management, increasingly tying workforce decisions to financial planning. Workday's Accounting Agent (close, revenue, payables, audit and analysis agents) is a lead example; Skyscanner says it cut management accounts from up to ten days to five.

> **A New CMO Takes the Helm Into the Loudest Possible Week** — Sarah Kennedy Ellis takes over global marketing and communications effective Oct. 5, reporting directly to Aneel Bhusri, after leading marketing for Google Cloud and, before that, Adobe Experience Cloud and Marketo. She inherits a narrative with three live wires: 525 roles cut in late September (~2.5% of staff, concentrated in Product & Technology, $65–80M in charges running into fiscal 2028), a brand-new Dubai hub opened four days later, and — still unresolved since August — reporting that Silver Lake has been in months-long talks to take the company private at a $43–51B valuation, among the largest software buyouts ever attempted. With founders Bhusri and Duffield controlling 68% of the vote, nothing moves without them, and no formal terms have surfaced. The Oct. 13 analyst day reconciled all of it, with Bhusri calling Rising a "re-founding moment"; Fortune's profile of Bhusri's "second act" notes Elliott Management's ~$2B stake is also in the picture.
